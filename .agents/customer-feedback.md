### 2026-05-20 Customer Research Run

**Research question:** Which business traveller or business travel-adjacent segment feels the strongest pain from planning UK multi-leg journeys, and is most likely to test EndMile now?

**Current answer:** The strongest validated pain is not "carbon reporting" on its own. It is the pre-travel comparison problem: working out a practical route when cost, timing, transfers, disruption, and expense rules all matter. Carbon remains a useful differentiator, but the early evidence suggests it should not be the only lead message.

**Evidence quality:** Directional only. The survey has 5 responses, all pre-launch, and does not yet include observed product usage. Do not build firm personas or pricing decisions from this data alone.

#### Top Themes Ranked By Frequency And Intensity

| Rank | Theme | Frequency | Intensity | Confidence | What it means |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multi-tool planning friction | 4 of 5 used 2-3 apps or websites | Medium | Medium | The "Planning a UK journey should not take 3 apps" message is worth continuing to test. |
| 2 | Timing and connection anxiety | 3 of 5 mentioned timing, connections, missed connections, or strikes | High | Medium | Journey risk should be explained with concrete examples: missed connections, strikes, long waits, awkward transfers. |
| 3 | Cost sensitivity before booking | 2 of 5 explicitly mentioned price pressure | Medium | Medium | Lead with "real cost before you travel", especially for charities, SMEs, public sector, universities, and budget-conscious teams. |
| 4 | Expense uncertainty and leakage | 2 of 5 had rejected, questioned, or unclaimed expenses | Medium | Medium | Expense confidence is a credible secondary angle, but needs more research with finance/admin buyers. |
| 5 | Receipt and claim admin | 3 of 5 spent 15+ minutes on expenses | Medium | Medium | This may support a future workflow product, but the immediate EndMile wedge should stay pre-travel route comparison. |

#### Jobs To Be Done

**Functional job:** Compare realistic UK business travel options before committing, including first mile, last mile, fares, driving, parking, transfers, disruption risk, and carbon.

**Emotional job:** Feel confident that the route chosen is reasonable, defensible, and not going to create avoidable cost, delays, or expense trouble.

**Social job:** Look like someone who makes sensible travel decisions: cost-aware, practical, reliable, and aligned with company expectations.

#### Best Early Segments To Test Next

| Segment | Why it fits | Risk | Suggested outreach angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consultants and client-facing professionals | Frequent business journeys, often multi-city, easy to understand the pain personally. | They may expense travel without caring about company-wide reporting. | "Would this have helped when planning client-site travel?" |
| University staff and departments | Staff, visitors, conferences, campuses, public-sector cost pressure, sustainability relevance. | Buying process may be slow. | "How do staff and visitors compare rail, driving, parking, and last-mile options?" |
| Event organisers | Repeated venue travel decisions, attendee/staff logistics, awkward last-mile planning. | They may care more about groups than individual business journeys. | "Could this help compare venue travel options before recommending routes?" |
| Business parks and regional offices | Last-mile access is often poor, car vs rail trade-offs matter. | They may be more place-based than journey-planning buyers. | "Could this help people choose practical routes to your site?" |
| Sustainability or ESG leads | Scope 3 travel angle and carbon comparison. | May not own travel behaviour or tools. | "Do route-level carbon comparisons help before travel decisions are made?" |

**Recommended first test:** Start with consultants, university staff, and event organisers. They are easier to reach than corporate travel managers and more likely to understand awkward journey planning immediately.

### 2026-10-04 Macro-Market Research Synthesis: Expanding the Consultant & Field Profile

**Research question:** Who else shares the travel planning and route checking behavior observed with our high-engagement organic user, and what are our viable acquisition and monetization options?

**Sample:** Analysis of observed telemetry from our organic IT consulting user cross-referenced against UK corporate travel, professional services, and field engineering market research.

**Key Findings:**
1. **Broader Ecosystem:** The need for daily/frequent multimodal UK route planning extends beyond software consultancies to:
   - Management & HR consultancies
   - Regional accountancy and legal audit teams
   - Field service engineering, utility maintenance, and construction commissioning teams
   - Public sector inspectorates and academic accreditation bodies
2. **Operational Roles & Job Titles (Mid-Market UK Firms, 50–300 staff):**
   - *No "Travel Managers":* Travel coordination falls to operational generalists: **Operations Coordinators, Practice Managers, Resource Managers, Project Support Officers (PSOs), and Executive Assistants (EAs)**.
   - *Daily Micro-Workflow:* Informal request via Slack/email -> manual collation across 3 tabs (Google Maps driving + Trainline rail + parking/taxi) -> summary pasted into Outlook/Slack -> costs entered into PSA/ERP tools (Kimble, Kantata, NetSuite) under client billing codes.
   - *Triggers for Daily 1–2 Searches:* Recurring weekly consultant client rotations, budget pre-approvals, and checking connection risks/strikes the day before travel.
3. **Core Economic & Regulatory Drivers:**
   - **HMRC 55p AMAP vs Rail TCO Arbitrage:** Mileage recharges (£110+ on a 200mi trip) trigger disputes when clients see cheap headline train fares on Trainline; EndMile proves the true door-to-door TCO (station parking + taxi + transit time).
   - **Duty of Care & Connection Risk:** Avoiding itineraries with fragile transfers or rail disruptions.
   - **Scope 3 Category 6 (DEFRA 2026):** Activity-based carbon accounting required for PPN 06/21 public tender compliance.
4. **Commercial Monetization Filter (Crucial Boundary):**
   - **High-Burn Vanity Traps (Rejected):** Multi-thousand-pound trade shows/conventions (e.g. Business Travel Show), PR in trade magazines (e.g. *Business Travel News Europe*), and expensive LinkedIn ads (£8-£15 CPC) have **no direct, cost-effective monetization path** for a £19–£49/mo SaaS.
   - **High-ROI Monetized Channels (Approved):** 
     - *Direct sniper cold outreach* to named Operations Coordinators, Practice Managers, and EAs.
     - *High-intent inbound search* targeting pain-led queries (*"train vs car cost calculator UK"*, *"fuel vs train fare calculator"*, *"55p per mile calculator UK"*, *"rail + taxi route cost UK"*).
     - *In-product PLG viral loops* (watermarked Pre-Trip PDF justification reports attached to client invoices, 1-click mobile itineraries dispatched to consultants).
     - *Purchasing friction:* Low (£19–£49/mo sits comfortably on coordinator corporate credit cards without IT procurement).
