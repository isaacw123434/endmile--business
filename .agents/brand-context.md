## Naming

- Use `EndMile` for product, UI, marketing copy, nav logos, CTAs, and casual user-facing references.
- Use `EndMile Routing` for legal, formal, metadata, company profile, and contact contexts.
- Never use `Endmile` or `End Mile`.

## Voice

Tone: direct, practical, business-focused.

Style:
- plain English
- concrete route trade-offs
- low fluff
- sharp but fair contrasts against partial tools
- early-stage and candid when asking for testers

Personality:
- useful
- precise
- pragmatic
- transparent

## Copy Rules

- Prefer British English.
- No em dashes.
- No exclamation marks in body copy.
- Avoid vague sustainability claims.
- Avoid fastest-only positioning.
- Avoid making EndMile sound like a generic consumer journey planner.
- Use route-level specifics: cost, time, risk, carbon, first mile, last mile, fares, parking, mileage, taxis, connections, expense rules.

## Core Message

EndMile helps businesses compare the full UK business journey before employees travel: door-to-door cost, time, journey risk, and carbon in one place.

## Social Message

The current strongest launch hook is:

```text
Planning a UK journey should not take 3 apps.
```

Use this as a top-of-funnel pain angle, especially on LinkedIn. For follow-up posts, move from launch announcement toward customer discovery and specific journey trade-offs.
