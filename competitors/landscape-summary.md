# Competitive Landscape for EndMile

The EndMile roadmap shows an MVP-level consumer app that compares multimodal journeys (driving, rail, taxi, bus, smart swap) and a routing engine with personal preference controls and live tracking. The company is pivoting toward B2B, focusing on pre-travel decision support (comparing cost, time, risk, carbon and policy), strengthening account foundations (tenant onboarding, SSO, policy guardrails, saved-route reports) and selecting a market wedge based on feedback. Current gaps include no validated buyer persona, immature admin portal and lack of company policy guardrails. Competitors in the mobility and corporate travel space span consumer MaaS apps, B2B mobility platforms, travel-expense management systems and large-scale mapping services. The following profiles summarise the positioning of key competitors relative to EndMile's strategy.

## Whim (MaaS Global)

Product & mission: Whim (MaaS Global, Finland) offered a MaaS app that let users plan, book and pay for trips combining public transport, bikes, e-scooters, ferries, taxis and rental cars in one subscription-or pay-as-you-go platform. The company aimed to offer a better alternative to owning a private car and pledged to replace 1 million private cars by 2030[1]. Whim's mission emphasised sustainable travel; since its launch in 2017 the service operated in Helsinki, Turku, Antwerp, Vienna and Tokyo and delivered more than 16 million trips[2]. Whim offered unlimited subscription plans and pay-as-you-go options that let customers book buses, trains, taxis, bikes and rental cars[3].

Recent developments: MaaS Global filed for bankruptcy in March 2024. The Sifted report notes that the company had raised over EUR 160 million, but financial losses led to insolvency and only around 10 000 active users in Helsinki remained[4]. Its technology and customer base were later acquired by another mobility platform (umob). The bankruptcy underscores the difficulty of building a profitable MaaS business reliant on consumer subscriptions.

Comparison to EndMile: Whim targeted urban consumers and municipal partners and relied on subscription packages. EndMile's B2B pivot avoids direct booking and focuses instead on decision support (cost/time/carbon/risk) and enterprise policy compliance, so Whim's failure illustrates the risk of scaling consumer-focused MaaS without a clear B2B revenue model. Whim's large multi-modal network and integrated payments show that consumers value convenience, but EndMile plans to delay booking and payment integration until a market wedge is chosen.

## SkedGo / TripGo

Product: SkedGo (Australia/Germany) provides a MaaS technology stack used by public-transport authorities, corporations and startups. Its intermodal routing engine integrates over 4 000 transport providers worldwide and offers features such as accessibility routing, occupancy information and personalisation options that prioritise low carbon, cost, speed or convenience[5]. The platform offers white-label apps, SDKs and APIs; clients can build customised journey planners, booking, parking and itinerary modules[6]. The company covers more than 500 cities/regions and handles more than 30 million API calls monthly[7]. It emphasises active travel and sustainable transport and has won multiple MaaS industry awards[8]. A case study for Leicester City Council notes that SkedGo's planner displays cost, calories burnt and carbon for journeys, and allows users to combine modes (e.g., cycling + bus + walking)[9].

Target market: System integrators, municipalities and corporate partners. Offers white-label solutions rather than consumer apps. Clients can customise policy rules, integrate with local ticketing and create branded apps.

Comparison to EndMile: Both focus on multi-modal journey planning and personalised routing. SkedGo already sells B2B white-label solutions with full booking and payment. EndMile's differentiator is in pre-travel decision justification-overlaying real-time cost, carbon, risk and policy for business travellers-and emphasising enterprise account features (tenant onboarding, policy guardrails, SSO and admin portal). SkedGo's experience and API breadth pose a competitive benchmark for coverage, accessibility, carbon-aware routing and B2B packaging, but using a competitor's full routing engine would weaken EndMile's core product position.

## You. Smart. Thing. (YST)

Product & positioning: YST is a travel demand-management platform designed for destinations, venues and live events. Its map-based travel assistant provides personalised door-to-door travel plans embedded into client websites and ticketing systems; it claims to have delivered over 13 million personal travel plans and to increase upsell conversions by ~20 %[10] while reducing carbon emissions by ~30 % through modal nudging[11]. Features include step-free routing and EV/active travel options (used by about 45 % of users)[12], live travel alerts with 67 % engagement[10] and configurable routing to specific venue access points that capture visitors' travel intent[13]. The service is web-based-no download is required-and can be embedded in booking or CRM systems[14].

Target market: Venues, cultural institutions, major events, local authorities and business parks looking to manage visitor flows, encourage sustainable travel and collect travel-demand data. YST emphasises that it adds personalisation layers on top of Google Maps so that destinations retain the customer relationship[14]. The platform also provides data for travel demand management, disruption handling and targeted marketing[13].

Comparison to EndMile: Both emphasise personalised multimodal routing and carbon reduction. YST focuses on events and destinations rather than corporate travel. EndMile aims to serve B2B travel managers by comparing business travel options across cost, time, risk and carbon, and integrating with expense and policy workflows. YST's success in increasing upsell conversions and engagement through personalisation and travel alerts shows the potential value of tailored communication and may inform EndMile's feedback-capture and route-sharing features.

## Mobilleo (Fleetondemand)

Product: Mobilleo is a business mobility-as-a-service platform launched by Fleetondemand in 2016. It offers a mobile-first app and web portal that enables employees to find, book and pay for all aspects of a business trip-including car hire, car share, taxis, rail, flights, hotels, airport lounges and parking-in a single itinerary. The platform features a six-way journey discovery engine and an end-to-end user experience, letting users assess multiple options for work-related journeys and view total cost of mobility data[15]. It includes policy management, fuel and predicted expense tools, local guides and social integration[15]. Journeys can originate from door-to-door search, calendar integration, email, on-demand booking, manual creation or preference-based rules[16].

Target market: Corporate mobility and fleet managers, large employers and car-leasing firms. Mobilleo positions itself as a complete booking and payment platform with dedicated account managers and data analytics (total cost of mobility). The service aims to reduce costs and encourage an open booking culture while still allowing company mobility objectives[17].

Comparison to EndMile: Mobilleo delivers full booking, payment, policy management and expense integration, whereas EndMile's current roadmap intentionally delays booking to focus on pre-travel decision support. EndMile can differentiate by offering deeper analytics on risk and carbon, while using an API or partnership for booking if needed. Mobilleo's six-way journey origin and smart mobility engine provide user convenience; EndMile must ensure its routing engine and UI are robust enough to support comparable complexity when shifting to B2B workflows.

## Navan (TripActions)

Product: Navan is a global AI-powered business travel and expense platform serving over 10 000 companies. It integrates travel booking, payments (virtual cards) and expense management into a single platform. The company claims a 96 % customer satisfaction score and 43 Net Promoter Score; it reported $613 million in trailing-twelve-month revenue and $7.6 billion in gross booking volume for the year ending July 31 2025[18]. Navan positions itself as an AI-driven alternative to legacy travel management companies.

Recent AI features: In May 2026 Navan announced new AI companions to help finance and travel admins analyse spend. The Travel Admin Companion lets admins ask plain-language questions to identify savings, benchmark against peers or block travel to specific destinations[19]. It supports "what-if" modelling to simulate policy changes[20]. The Expense Admin Companion analyses flagged transactions and drafts context-aware messages to employees[21]. A Book with AI feature allows travellers to book a trip using a single prompt ("I need to be in London next Tuesday")-the system suggests personalised, policy-compliant options and completes booking within the chat[22]. Users can also expense with video and voice by recording a receipt and narration; the AI extracts OCR data and auto-populates expense reports[23]. Navan reports that about 73 % of expenses are automatically approved; the new features target the remaining transactions[24].

Comparison to EndMile: Navan is a mature, full-stack travel and expense platform with deep AI features and large enterprise adoption. It competes directly in the business travel management space that EndMile is considering. Navan's integrated booking, payment and expense automation means EndMile must find a narrower wedge (e.g., pre-trip comparison and sustainability analytics) where Navan's offering is less robust. Aligning with the roadmap, EndMile should focus on door-to-door cost/carbon/risk comparisons and policy guardrails rather than replicating Navan's extensive booking and expense features.

## Google Maps Platform (Routes API)

Product: Google Maps Platform provides multi-modal directions (transit, biking, driving, two-wheel motor vehicles or walking), real-time traffic data and tolls information. Developers can also optimise up to 25 waypoints to produce efficient routes[25]. The Routes API is widely used by third-party mobility and delivery services.

Target market: Developers building consumer or business apps requiring routing, mapping and geospatial data. Google does not provide direct travel expense or policy management features.

Comparison to EndMile: EndMile already uses its own routing engine across rail, driving, taxi, bus and Smart Swap and emphasises personal preferences and carbon/risk. Google Maps offers broad coverage and reliability but lacks domain-specific features like company travel policy evaluation or cost estimation. EndMile may consider integrating Google data for base mapping while differentiating through business-specific analytics, personalisation and sustainability metrics.

## SAP Concur

Product: SAP Concur is an established spend management platform that automates travel booking, expense reporting and invoice processing. Reviews note that it allows users to capture receipts via mobile app, submit expense reports easily, provides real-time visibility into spend, integrates with financial systems, and offers strong policy compliance and audit features[26]. A Global Business Travel Association article (2025) explains that new AI-powered agents within Concur help automate expense report creation and receipt validation[27]. At SAP Concur Fusion 2026, the company showcased AI agents such as the Expense Automation Agent that creates expense reports and populates custom fields automatically, and the Expense Pre-Submit Audit Agent that flags discrepancies early[28]. Concur is also integrating its copilot Joule with Microsoft 365 so employees can create and submit expense reports or book travel without leaving their usual applications[29].

Target market: Medium and large enterprises needing a single platform to manage travel bookings, expense reimbursement and invoice processing. Concur commands roughly half of the corporate travel and expense management market[30].

Comparison to EndMile: Concur focuses on post-booking travel expense management and integrates travel booking with corporate policies. EndMile's roadmap emphasises pre-trip decision support and route comparison rather than expense reimbursement. However, features like automated policy enforcement and AI-driven receipt analysis highlight the importance of compliance and automation in enterprise environments-capabilities EndMile may need to integrate or partner with in later phases.

## Citymapper

Product: Citymapper is a consumer-oriented urban mobility app that provides journey planning, real-time transit data and multimodal routing for major cities worldwide. The company's tagline is "Making Cities Usable"[31]. In March 2026, Citymapper (owned by Via) introduced AI-powered journey planning: when a user plans a trip, the app presents AI recommendations based on historical travel preferences and provides summaries of the trade-offs between routes-speed, cost, walking distance, real-time disruption and reliability[32]. These features aim to offer a personalised travel companion, moving beyond simple route finding. Citymapper also partners with cities to provide rider insights and tools to optimise transport networks[31].

Target market: Urban commuters and transit agencies. Citymapper's revenue comes from advertising, data partnerships and its "Pass" subscription (travel card). It does not provide corporate expense management.

Comparison to EndMile: Citymapper's AI features demonstrate the trend toward personalised, trade-off-aware routing-similar to EndMile's goal of comparing cost, time, risk and carbon. However, Citymapper remains consumer-focused and lacks enterprise features such as policy controls, saved-route reports or tenant onboarding. For B2B clients, EndMile can differentiate by offering customised policies, exportable route evidence and integration with finance systems.

## Ride East Midlands / West Midlands App

Product: Ride is a publicly funded multi-operator travel app for Nottingham and Derby, launched in December 2025 under the UK Future Transport Zones programme. By March 2026 it had over 12 600 downloads[33]. The app lets users plan trips across buses, trams, trains, Lime e-bikes and Dott e-scooters. It simplifies journeys by integrating ticket purchasing for buses and trams and allows payment for e-bike and e-scooter trips[34]. Users can track buses in real time, tailor the app to preferred modes or operators and receive departure guidance showing when to leave to catch a bus or collect an e-bike[35]. Tickets are stored in a digital wallet, and users can choose between the most cost-effective or fastest route[36]. Planned updates include accessibility features (step-free routing), deeper ticket integration, service change notifications and real-time traffic updates[37].

Target market: Residents, students and visitors in the East Midlands. The app is operated by the East Midlands Combined County Authority and aims to reduce carbon emissions by encouraging seamless multi-modal journeys[38]. Councils also use anonymised app data to inform transport planning[39].

Comparison to EndMile: Ride is a public transport app with integrated ticketing and localised coverage. It is not a B2B platform and lacks features such as company policy controls or expense reporting. However, its success (tens of thousands of downloads) and the government's endorsement as a model for transport apps illustrate the demand for user-friendly, real-time journey planning and integrated payment. EndMile can learn from Ride's user-experience design-real-time tracking, digital ticket wallet, and cost vs speed options-and adapt these concepts to business travel contexts.

## Implications for EndMile

Differentiate through decision support and sustainability analytics: Many competitors provide booking and multi-modal routing (Mobilleo, SkedGo, Navan, Perk, FCM Travel, Egencia, Routespring) or consumer and civic travel planning (Citymapper, Ride, Transit App, Trafi, Umo, Moovit, Meep). EndMile's roadmap emphasises comparing and justifying business travel decisions-weighing cost, time, risk and carbon, and aligning with company policy. Maintaining this focus, rather than full booking, can help EndMile occupy a niche not fully served by booking-centric platforms.

Build robust B2B foundations early: Competitors such as Navan, SAP Concur, Perk, FCM Travel, Egencia and Routespring highlight the importance of enterprise features: tenant onboarding, role-based access, SSO, policy management, audit logs, duty of care, approval flows, support and compliance automation. EndMile's roadmap correctly prioritises these shared foundations (tenant onboarding, SSO, policy guardrails) before pursuing a specific wedge.

Leverage selective partnerships and data enrichment: EndMile could partner with established booking and expense platforms (Mobilleo, Navan, SAP Concur, Perk, FCM Travel, Egencia, Routespring) where booking or expense workflows become necessary. Google Maps may be useful for mapping, traffic, tolls, fallback geospatial data or other narrow enrichment needs, but EndMile's own routing and decision engine should remain the core product rather than being replaced by a competitor routing platform.

Consider event and destination markets as secondary wedges: You. Smart. Thing.'s success in venues and events shows that travel demand management for large gatherings is a viable market. If EndMile's travel-comparison engine proves valuable, similar applications (universities, event organisers) could become an early wedge before tackling corporate travel management.

Monitor market viability: The collapse of MaaS Global (Whim) demonstrates the financial risk of consumer subscription models. EndMile should test pricing models and buyer personas early, as planned, to avoid scaling without a clear revenue path.

## Conclusion

EndMile operates in a competitive landscape that includes consumer MaaS apps, enterprise mobility platforms, full-stack travel-expense systems, travel management companies and routing infrastructure providers. Established players like Navan, SAP Concur, Perk, FCM Travel and Egencia have extensive enterprise workflows and service depth, while specialised platforms like SkedGo, Mobilleo, Trafi, Umo, Moovit and Meep show how broad the MaaS and white-label mobility market already is. Event-focused providers like You. Smart. Thing. and local apps like Ride and Transit App show demand for personalised, real-time travel guidance and integrated ticketing. To carve out a sustainable position, EndMile should emphasise its unique capability to compare, justify and improve business travel decisions-integrating carbon, cost, time and risk-while building robust B2B foundations and exploring partnerships for booking, payment and expense management.
